# Faster answers without worse ones (2026-09-30)

Two attempts at speed, each with a quality gate:
- **Restaurant answers in fewer words:** failed the gate, not shipped.
- **An engine cache for the fixed instructions:** works; see the end for the one thing that is
  not the same as before.

## 1. Restaurant answers in fewer words: not shipped

After [2026-09-29-places-answers.md](2026-09-29-places-answers.md) a restaurant answer takes
about two minutes on the phone: the model writes about 450 tokens at about 5 a second. Most of
it looked like padding: hours spelled out in words, an opening sentence, filler. Three prompts
over the same lines were run on the VM on the 20 restaurant questions (`scripts/places_answers.py`;
`eval/answers_vm_places_{current,compact,six_slots,six}.jsonl`):

| Prompt | Tokens written (median) | Places named (median) |
|---|---|---|
| Current: six to eight places, a sentence or two each | 440 | 7 |
| Compact: one sentence each, hours as the list writes them, no opening | 411 | 8 |
| Six places, one line each | 329 | 6 |
| Six places, one natural sentence each, what the list lacks left out | 322 | 6 |

- **Compact:** shortens each place but names more of them, so the answer is barely shorter.
- **Six places, one line each:** fills slots even when they are empty ("serves unspecified
  cuisine", "The travel guide says: No additional description provided in the list").
- **Six places in natural sentences:** graded blind, side by side with the current answers and the
  web reference, by one grader (`eval/sets_places_six.json`, key and grades alongside):

| | Share of the reference | Errors |
|---|---|---|
| Current | 60% | 55 |
| Six places | 52% | 41 |

It was worse on 14 of 20 questions and better on 2, and it answered the Portuguese question in
English again. The graders reward places and details, and the words the model saves are
the details. The current prompt stays.

## 2. The engine keeps its state after the fixed instructions

Every step of a question sends the engine a prompt that starts with the same instructions (the
planning prompt, the answer-first prompt), about 100-130 tokens, so the phone re-reads them at
about 25 tokens a second each time.

Engine patch `patches/0007-bigmoeonedge-prefix-cache.patch` (`--prefix-cache N`; a request's
`cache_prefix` says how many bytes of its prompt are fixed):
- **How:** after a prompt's fixed part is read, the engine copies the model's state into one of N
  spare sequences. The next prompt that starts with the same tokens copies it back and reads only
  the rest.
- **Why a copy:** Qwen3.6's recurrent layers cannot drop part of a sequence, so the state is
  copied rather than trimmed.
- **Memory layout:** llama.cpp shares a recurrent cell between sequences until one of them
  decodes, then copies it. The attention cache is put in one buffer, so its cells can be shared
  too.
- **Room for each turn:** the least recently used copies are dropped when a turn needs the room.
- **Stale copies:** a copy whose sequence no longer ends where its prompt does is ignored.

**Measured at engine level on the Pixel** (the app's engine settings, 7 requests like the app's:
two general questions and two restaurant questions; `eval/engine_prefix/`):

| Request | Without | With (4 copies) |
|---|---|---|
| Planning, second question | 7.5-9.1 s reading, 126 tokens | 2.9 s, 23 tokens |
| Answer-first draft, second question | 7.0-9.2 s, 105 tokens | 1.6 s, 23 tokens |
| Restaurants, second city | 33-35 s, 775 tokens | 25.7 s, 526 tokens |
| Restaurants, first city | 27.6-28.8 s | **38 s** |

**The restaurant miss:** reading the instructions and the rest as two passes makes the engine read
most of the expert weights from flash twice.

**What ships:**
- **Short tails only:** a prompt is copied only when at most 256 tokens follow its fixed part, which
  covers planning, answer-first drafts, translation and follow-ups. Long prompts (restaurants,
  sources-first answers) are read in one pass exactly as before.
- **Two copies** (`AppSettings.PREFIX_CACHE`).
- **Memory:** the lowest free memory during the run was 0.90 GB, as without the cache (0.89-1.02 GB).
  With four copies it was 0.75-0.79 GB.

**In the app**, four general questions in one session:

The phone ran the app with two copies. The first question came from a cold start, and each next
question went to the running app:

| Question | Planning | First words of the answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 "Why did the Roman Empire split into East and West?" | 9.6 s (123 tokens read) | 14.6 s |
| 2 "How do vaccines train the immune system?" | 2.9 s (17 tokens) | **3.7 s** |
| 3 "What causes the seasons on Earth?" | 3.3 s (16 tokens) | draft started at 11.7 s |
| 4 "Why is the sky blue?" | 2.9 s (15 tokens) | **3.7 s** |

- **The draft:** read 15-17 tokens instead of 102.
- **First words:** from the second question on, an answer-first question shows them after about 4 s
  instead of about 15 s.
- **Unchanged:** the source check and the writing are the same as before.
- **Sources-first questions:** save the planning step, about 6 s.
- **Restaurant questions:** they have no planning step and their prompt is long, so they are unchanged.
- **The next bottleneck (question 3):** the route waited until 11.7 s. The first planned title
  ("Earth's orbit") is not an exact article title, so looking it up takes a full-text title search
  of several seconds. That used to finish while the planning was still being written (about 10 s)
  and now has nothing to hide behind.

### What is not the same as before

With the cache on, every answer can be worded slightly differently, including answers whose
prompts are never copied. Why: the engine's attention cache becomes one buffer shared by several
sequences, and reading the instructions in their own pass changes the order of floating-point
additions. Some examples:
- "the largest and most economically powerful city" became "the largest and most populous city";
- "because it transfers existing thermal energy" became "by transferring existing thermal energy";
- a planned title "Refrigeration cycle" became "Thermodynamic cycle".

What was checked:
- **Stability:** each mode is exactly repeatable (7 of 7 answers identical over two runs of each).
- **Same build:** the new engine with the cache off gives byte-identical answers to the shipped
  engine (7 of 7).

So the difference is the arithmetic of the cache. It is the same computation, so no systematic
change in quality is expected, but it is not byte-identical, and no blind grading of the new
wording has been done.
