## Reading

- Planned retrieval is a clear step up from naive retrieval (+1.0) and fixes multihop, but it
  still loses to closed-book by 1.2 and never beat it by 2 or more points on any question.
  A 35B model already knows this eval's head-of-distribution material; sources mostly acted
  as a distraction or a cage.
- What retrieval does deliver: about a quarter of the hallucinated items, and citations that
  the grader found mostly trustworthy (3 ungrounded answers, 2 wrong indices).
- Losses of 2+ points came from: fuzzy title resolution ("Rent control" -> "Rent control in
  Massachusetts", "Port city" -> "Port City Colombo", apostrophe variants), sections that
  missed the asked-for aspect (treatment, prevention, recession of the Dead Sea), the model
  ignoring relevant late-ranked passages, and refusing parts of the question instead of
  falling back on its own knowledge.
- The eval set under-rewards retrieval: it has no long-tail entities, exact figures or
  post-training-cutoff facts, and the grader scores from its own memory.

## Decisions for v2

1. Answer first from the model's own knowledge (clean closed-book prompt, no citation talk),
   then run retrieval as a checking pass that adds corrections and sourced specifics. This
   keeps the closed-book quality floor and gives a first token in seconds rather than a minute.
2. Redirect-aware title resolution (`scripts/build_redirects.py`), reject fuzzy title hits.
3. Extend the eval with long-tail and exact-figure questions where closed-book should fail.
