# Heat and first-question speed (1.8.5)

2026-10-20/24, Pixel 8 Pro (GrapheneOS, Android 17), Qwen3.6-35B-A3B. Two changes are kept:
the status line's spinner is replaced by a ring that steps twice a second, and opening the app
reads the first steps' instructions into the prefix cache ahead of the first question. Two other
changes were measured and dropped: Android's "prefer power efficiency" performance hint for the
engine's threads, and longer library passages for Ethereum and cryptography questions.

| Change | Measured on | Result | Kept |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ring instead of a spinning indicator | 12 questions in one session, from 30 °C | 5.3% less time, 6.8% less energy, app CPU -36%, the same answers word for word | yes |
| Instructions read at app open | 4 first questions, with and without, alternating | first words 4.3 s instead of 13.7 s (answer-first route), whole answer 11% sooner | yes |
| "Prefer power efficiency" hint on the engine's threads | 2 questions | writing 1.5 instead of about 7.7 tokens/s, 6.5-7 min per question instead of about 2 | no |
| Library passages up to 1,000 characters instead of 650 | 20 crypto questions on the VM, 2 blind graders | 9 answers changed, scored lower by both graders (50 vs 48, 52.5 vs 48.5) | no |

## Where the power went

`scripts/heat_report.py` splits a session by question: the phone's power rails (perfetto's
`android.power` data source, read by `scripts/power_rails.py`), CPU seconds per thread of the app
and of the engine, each core cluster's busy share, clocks, skin temperature and the memory
counters, from `scripts/heat_sampler.sh` running on the phone next to `scripts/phone_session.sh`.

With 1.8.4, after the first answer the phone is hot and speed is set by its heat. The engine uses
about 4.3 cores while it writes, and the rest of the power was the app's own:

- The indeterminate `CircularProgressIndicator` next to the status line is an endless animation:
  it kept the screen redrawing at the display's full rate (up to 120 frames a second) for the whole
  of every answer, while the answer's text changes four times a second (`UI_FRAME_MS`). The little
  cores were 28-34% busy throughout, mostly with the app's render thread and the compositor.
- Android 17's per-app memory limit (6 GiB here) was reached 9 times per session, each followed
  by re-reads of evicted pages from flash. The questions after such an event were not slower,
  so this was left as it is.

## The ring

`TickingRing` (`ResearchScreen.kt`) fills by an eighth every half second, two frames a second.
The same 12 questions in one session (`scripts/phone_session.sh`), each session from a phone at
30 °C at full charge:

| | Spinner (1.8.4) | Ring |
|---|---|---|
| Time for the 12 questions | 1,535 s | **1,453 s** (-5.3%) |
| Energy for the 12 questions | 6,363 J | **5,933 J** (-6.8%) |
| App CPU | 490 s | **315 s** (-36%) |
| Little cores busy during answers | 28-34% | 19-29% |
| Other rails (GPU and display pipeline), per question | 574-678 mW | 460-561 mW |
| Skin temperature after the 12th question | 44.2 °C | 43.8 °C |

Every question was faster, and all 12 answers were the same word for word. The engine computes
exactly what it did; over the questions the big core averaged 2,013 MHz instead of 1,982 and the
middle cores the same 1,541 MHz.

## Instructions read at app open

The planner's and the draft's instructions are the same for every question, and from the second
question on they come from the prefix cache (patch 0007). The first question of a session read
them itself. Now `RunService.warmPrefixes` reads both into the cache once the model has loaded
(about 11 s, nothing on screen), so the first question reads only itself, as later ones do. A
question asked meanwhile waits for the request being read and the rest is skipped.

`scripts/phone_first_question.sh` opens the app, waits 90 s as someone typing would, and asks;
each question once with the warm-up and once without (`ANDROIDLM_WARM=0`), alternating which goes
first, each from a fresh app at 31.5 °C:

| Question | First words, with / without | Planning, with / without | Done, with / without |
|---|---|---|---|
| Earthquake in a hotel room | 4.9 / 14.4 s | 3.5 / 9.3 s | 84 / 98 s |
| Hypothermia in a hiking partner | 4.6 / 14.1 s | 3.2 / 9.1 s | 111 / 123 s |
| Paying by card in Tbilisi | 3.3 / 12.6 s | 2.0 / 7.6 s | 81 / 91 s |
| Stateful hash-based signatures | 47.0 / 42.5 s | 3.4 / 9.3 s | 71 / 78 s |

The last question is answered from its sources (the Ethereum and cryptography library), so its
first words come after the search; the first search of a fresh app reads Wikipedia's index from
flash (14-19 s), and with planning done sooner the question waited for it. Its answer was read in
28 s in both runs, and it finished 7 s sooner.

## Dropped: the power-efficiency hint

An optional engine change (`BMOE_ADPF=efficient`, not in `patches/`) put the decoding thread and
the pool's workers in an Android performance hint session marked "prefer power efficiency"
(API 35). The session was created and the mark accepted (`adpf: mode=efficient session=yes
threads=5 prefer_efficiency=on`), and the Pixel's power HAL then capped the threads' clocks: the
draft was written at 1.5 tokens/s and the source check at 0.9-1.0, and a question took 392-412 s
instead of about 120-130 s. The session was stopped after two questions.

## Dropped: longer library passages

On "Which signature algorithms are quantum resistant?" (cry-001), answers have called FN-DSA
(Falcon) standardized; FIPS 206 is still a draft. The library has the facts: NIST IR 8547's
section on signature algorithms lists ML-DSA, SLH-DSA, LMS and XMSS and says a Falcon FIPS is to
come. But the context cuts every passage at 650 characters, which drops the LMS/XMSS sentences
of that 1,119-character passage. Giving library passages the 1,000-character limit of Wikipedia's
leads was tried on the 20 crypto questions on the VM (llama-server, the phone's one-message format,
greedy), and graded blind by two graders next to the web reference
(`eval/sets_vm_packlong_cry.json`, `key_vm_packlong_cry.json`, `grades_vm_packlong_cry_g1.json`,
`_g2.json`; answers `eval/answers_vm_packlong_{650,1000}.jsonl`):

| | 650 characters (shipped) | 1,000 characters |
|---|---|---|
| Share of the web reference, grader 1 | 55% | 54% |
| Share of the web reference, grader 2 | 56% | 54% |
| The 9 changed answers, grader 1 / grader 2 | 50 / 52.5 | 48 / 48.5 |

The quantum-signatures answer gained XMSS and LMS but added Rainbow as quantum resistant (it was
broken in 2022), and the other changed answers were scored lower. On the VM the shipped setting
did not repeat the FN-DSA mistake, so on that question it varies from run to run.
